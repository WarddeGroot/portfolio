Back-to-school onboarding

After the summer break, every teacher sets up Gynzy for a new school year. I turned that moment into a short onboarding flow that tells us who we're designing for, nudges schools to connect Basispoort, and cleans up duplicate accounts along the way.

Why the start of the school year matters

  • After the summer break, teachers return to a new class, sometimes a new grade, and set up Gynzy for the rest of the year. It's the one moment where almost every teacher is open to configuring things.
  • At the same time, we knew surprisingly little about our users. We didn't reliably know which grade a teacher taught, or which math and spelling method their school used.
  • That made it hard to personalize the Start page, to target new features at the right teachers, and to trust our own data.

The goals

  • Personalize: serve teachers better on the Start page, based on their grade and methods
  • Reach the right teachers: approach teachers in a more personal way when we launch future features
  • Raise data quality: fewer duplicate accounts, more schools connected to Basispoort
  • Keep it light: teachers are busy in the first weeks, so every step had to be quick and skippable

The flow

Instead of a long form, I designed a series of short steps. Each one asks a single question, and the flow adapts to who you are: a regular teacher sees different steps than a school administrator.

1. Which grade do you teach this year?

  • Teachers often switch grades between school years, so we ask again every year instead of relying on old data
  • The grade directly shapes which content we can highlight on the Start page

2. Which methods do you use for math and spelling?

  • Dutch schools work with a variety of teaching methods. Knowing which one a teacher uses lets us align content and features with their daily lessons
  • It also tells us which methods matter most to our users, valuable input for the product roadmap

3. Connecting Basispoort

Basispoort is the platform Dutch primary schools use to connect educational software to their student administration system. Once a school links Gynzy to Basispoort, classes, students and teachers are synced automatically, and everyone can sign in with a single account. Without that link, teachers have to create and maintain classes by hand, which leads to outdated groups and duplicate accounts.

The catch: only a school administrator can set up the link. Most teachers aren't administrators, so the step had to work differently depending on who you are.

  • Administrators get a clear explanation of what the link brings them and are nudged to set it up right away
  • Teachers without admin rights get a ready-made email to their administrator, explaining why and how to connect Basispoort. One click and it's on its way, so the teacher doesn't have to figure out what to write

4. Merging duplicate accounts

  • Some teachers ended up with two accounts: one created by a sync, and one they had made themselves earlier. Their lessons and settings were split across both
  • When we detect a possible duplicate, the flow offers to merge the accounts, so the teacher continues with one account and all their work in one place
  • For us, every merge means cleaner data and fewer support questions

Building the UI with AI

Besides designing the flow, I built almost all of its UI components myself, using AI. Instead of handing over designs and waiting for implementation, I could take a component from design to working code and iterate on the real thing.

  • Less back-and-forth between design and development, and more developer time for the logic behind the flow
  • Design details ended up in the product as intended, because I built them
  • It changed my role: from delivering screens to delivering working components

Results

  • In August and September 2026, more than 86,000 teachers started the onboarding.
  • 86.5% saved their grade. For over 74,000 teachers, we now know which grade they teach this school year.
  • More than 58,000 teachers (68%) completed the full flow, including their math and spelling method.
  • The three peaks follow the staggered regional summer holidays: every time a region went back to school, thousands of teachers started the flow on the first days. Proof that the timing was right.
  • The biggest drop-off happens after the grade step. That's exactly where the next iteration comes in: letting teachers finish later from their Start page.
