Back-to-school onboarding

After the summer break, every teacher sets up Gynzy for a new school year. I turned that moment into a short onboarding flow that tells us who we're designing for, nudges schools to connect Basispoort, and cleans up duplicate accounts along the way.

Why the start of the school year matters

  • After the summer break, teachers return to a new class, sometimes a new grade, and set up Gynzy for the rest of the year. It's the one moment where almost every teacher is open to configuring things.
  • At the same time, we knew surprisingly little about our users. We didn't reliably know which grade a teacher taught, or which math and spelling method their school used.
  • That made it hard to personalize the Start page, to target new features at the right teachers, and to trust our own data.

The goals

  • Personalize: serve teachers better on the Start page, based on their grade and methods
  • Reach the right teachers: approach teachers in a more personal way when we launch future features
  • Raise data quality: fewer duplicate accounts, more schools connected to Basispoort
  • Keep it light: teachers are busy in the first weeks, so every step had to be quick and skippable

The approach

We knew there was a lot to do before the start of the school year. So instead of starting with screens, I started with the question of who we were designing for.

Starting with data

  • I mapped the segments within the product and looked at which indicators best predict activity in Gynzy
  • Only very few teachers had filled in their grade and method, while we consider exactly that information essential for personalizing Gynzy, now and in the future
  • In the weeks before the school year starts, teachers already log in to set up all their systems again, Gynzy included. They have the time for it then. If they don't do it at that moment, chances are they never will

Choosing who to serve first

  • We split users by role (administrator or not), whether they're connected to a school, and whether that school has a Basispoort link
  • Teachers at a school with a Basispoort link turned out to be the most active, and they keep using Gynzy. That made them the most important group to reach
  • Next came teachers who had set up a school, but no Basispoort link yet
  • Teachers without a school were deliberately left out. The flow for adding a school already existed elsewhere, but not yet in Flutter, and we could still point them to it during the year

Where it got hard

  • Designing the screens was the easy part. The real challenge was untangling all the segments, at the speed the start of the school year demanded
  • Merging accounts took the most time. The flow also had to work in another part of the app, so it had to be set up in a scalable way
  • The matching logic was tricky: teachers with the same email domain at the same school, or with the same first or last name. We had to tell them apart without leaking other people's accounts into the UI
  • That flow came with lots of edge cases, and we had to course-correct several times along the way

The flow

Instead of a long form, I designed a series of short steps. Each one asks a single question, and the flow adapts to the teacher's situation: steps without anything to show drop out, and a school without a Basispoort link gets a different step than a school that already has one.

The starting point: a personal welcome after the summer break

1. Merging duplicate accounts

  • Some teachers ended up with two accounts: one created by a sync, and one they had made themselves earlier. Their lessons and settings were split across both
  • When we detect possible duplicates, the flow shows them side by side, including how many lessons each account holds. The teacher picks which ones are theirs and confirms each one via email, so nobody can claim someone else's account
  • For us, every merge means cleaner data and fewer support questions

2. Connecting Basispoort

Basispoort is the platform Dutch primary schools use to connect educational software to their student administration system. Once a school links Gynzy to Basispoort, classes, students and teachers are synced automatically, and everyone can sign in with a single account. Without that link, teachers have to create and maintain classes by hand, which leads to outdated groups and duplicate accounts.

The catch: only a school administrator can set up the link. Most teachers aren't administrators, so the step had to work differently depending on who you are.

  • Not linked yet? The step explains in three short points what the link brings: all groups and students in Gynzy automatically, changes visible right away, and no costs
  • Administrators get a clear explanation of what the link brings them and are nudged to set it up right away
  • Teachers without admin rights get a ready-made message for their administrator, explaining why and how to connect Basispoort, with a link to the step-by-step guide. One click copies it, so the teacher doesn't have to figure out what to write
  • Already linked? Then this step turns into a quick check of the groups we pulled in from Basispoort

3. Which grades do you teach this year?

  • Teachers often switch grades between school years, so we ask again every year instead of relying on old data
  • The grade directly shapes which content we can highlight on the Start page
  • Combination classes are common in Dutch schools, so teachers can pick more than one grade

4. Which methods do you use for math and spelling?

  • Dutch schools work with a variety of teaching methods. Knowing which one a teacher uses lets us align content and features with their daily lessons
  • It also tells us which methods matter most to our users, valuable input for the product roadmap

Building the UI with AI

To take work off the development team's plate under that time pressure, I built almost all of the UI components I designed myself, using AI. Instead of handing over designs and waiting for implementation, I could take a component from design to working code and iterate on the real thing.

  • Less back-and-forth between design and development, and more developer time for the logic behind the flow
  • Design details ended up in the product as intended, because I built them
  • It changed my role: from delivering screens to delivering working components

Results

  • In August and September 2026, more than 86,000 teachers started the onboarding.
  • 86.5% saved their grade. For over 74,000 teachers, we now know which grade they teach this school year.
  • More than 58,000 teachers (68%) completed the full flow, including their math and spelling method.
  • Teachers sent almost 3,700 merge requests. More than 1,200 duplicate accounts have already been merged, and another 2,277 requests are waiting for email confirmation.
  • The three peaks follow the staggered regional summer holidays: every time a region went back to school, thousands of teachers started the flow on the first days. Proof that the timing was right.
  • The biggest drop-off happens after the grade step. That's exactly where the next iteration comes in: letting teachers finish later from their Start page.
